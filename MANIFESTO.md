# The 198x Manifesto

## Why 198x exists

Modern computing did not appear overnight.

It is the result of countless people solving countless problems over many decades.

Some became famous.

Most did not.

Together they built the foundations that every computer, game, operating system, programming language and application now stands on.

Those giants were ordinary people doing their best.

They worked with limited knowledge, limited budgets and limited time.

They made mistakes. They made compromises. They argued, experimented, borrowed ideas, and found better ones.

They were engineers, artists, musicians, designers, scientists, mathematicians and curious people solving the problems in front of them.

198x exists to understand their work.

Not because it belongs in a museum.

Because it still has something to teach us.

---

## Curiosity

This project is not about retro computing.

It is not even about computers.

It is about curiosity.

Computers are one of the richest places in human history for asking "why?"

Every machine, every game, every operating system, every compiler, every CPU, every language, every circuit, every abstraction exists because somebody faced a problem and tried to solve it.

198x exists to explore those decisions.

Everything begins with a question.

> Why?

Then:

> How?

Then:

> Why this way?

Then:

> What else did people try?

Then:

> What happened next?

Then:

> What if...?

Eventually:

> What would you build?

Curiosity is not a distraction from learning.

It is learning.

The purpose of education is not to supply answers.

It is to help people ask better questions.

---

## Follow the thread

Every answer leads somewhere.

A question about a Spectrum game may lead to memory layout.

Memory leads to processor architecture.

Processor architecture leads to digital logic.

Digital logic leads to transistors.

Transistors lead to semiconductor manufacturing.

Semiconductor manufacturing leads to physics.

A thread might instead run through game design, music, graphics, mathematics, psychology, economics or history.

Nothing exists in isolation.

The destination is not predetermined.

The value lies in following the thread.

Curiosity suggests the route; it does not decide what the project takes on. Which threads are worth pulling, and when to stop pulling one, is a working question — see `PRACTICE.md`.

---

## Understanding

198x is interested in constructive understanding.

Not knowing that something exists.

Understanding it well enough to explain it.

To recreate it.

To improve it.

To question it.

To teach it.

To build upon it.

Facts matter.

Understanding matters more.

Every abstraction has a history.

Every system has layers beneath it.

Understanding those layers gives better judgement about the layers above.

---

## Constraints

Constraints are teachers.

Engineering is the art of making good decisions within them.

Old computers make them visible, because they cannot hide them.

Memory.

Processing power.

Storage.

Colour.

Sound.

Cost.

Time.

Modern computers hide most of those behind extraordinary abstractions.

Understanding the old constraints helps in understanding the new ones.

The technology changes.

The engineering rarely does.

---

## History

History is not a museum.

History is engineering in progress.

Every historical machine is a frozen set of design decisions.

It is not a list of winners, either.

Many good ideas disappeared.

Many imperfect ideas became standards.

Many machines failed commercially while changing the future.

Computing was never inevitable.

People made decisions, and those decisions had consequences.

198x exists to understand that work, not to mythologise it.

---

## Preservation

Preservation is more than saving hardware.

We preserve:

- behaviour
- documentation
- tools
- engineering decisions
- trade-offs
- experiments
- conversations
- context

Understanding is what we are preserving.

---

## Knowledge is connected

198x does not only collect facts.

It collects relationships.

Questions connect to answers.

Answers connect to ideas.

Ideas connect to people.

People connect to history.

History connects to modern computing.

Programming connects to mathematics, mathematics to electronics, electronics to manufacturing, manufacturing to economics, economics to design, design to psychology, psychology to games, games to culture.

The interesting part is rarely the individual fact.

It is the path between them.

Real problems do not respect academic departments.

Neither should curiosity.

---

## Creation

Preservation serves understanding.

Understanding serves creation.

Each is necessary. None is sufficient alone.

We do not study historical systems to recreate them forever.

We study them to understand the problems they solved.

Historical games, machines and techniques are examples.

The goal is never imitation.

Every generation inherits ideas from the one before, and adds something of its own.

Not by copying.

By continuing the conversation.

---

## Judgement

Technology changes.

Frameworks change.

Languages change.

Tools change.

Judgement endures.

198x is not trying to produce experts in obsolete technology, nor to teach whichever tools are currently fashionable.

It is trying to cultivate a way of thinking.

To ask better questions.

To understand systems deeply.

To see connections.

To appreciate trade-offs.

To respect the people who came before us.

---

## Continue the conversation

We preserve because knowledge can be lost.

We teach because knowledge grows when it is shared.

We create because every generation deserves the chance to contribute something new.

The future is not predetermined.

It is built by ordinary people solving difficult problems, as it always has been.

198x exists to help more people become part of that story.
